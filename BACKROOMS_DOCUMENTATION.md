# The Backrooms: Complete Documentation

## Overview

The Backrooms is a collaborative fictional universe about an impossibly large extradimensional complex of empty rooms accessed by "noclipping" through reality. It originated in a 2019 4chan post and has since evolved into a massive community-driven worldbuilding project with multiple wiki communities contributing to the lore.

## Core Concept

**What are the Backrooms?**
- An infinite labyrinth of liminal spaces (uncannily familiar architectural spaces that feel "off")
- Accessed accidentally by exiting reality in certain locations
- Organized into numbered levels and anomalous levels
- Each level has distinct visual, audio, and physical characteristics
- Inhabited by various entities and mutated humans
- Extremely difficult to escape from

**The First Law of Backrooms:** *"Don't go further."* Most people who explore too deep never return.

## Level 0: "The Threshold" (The Yellow Hell)

### Description
The entry level and often the last level most wanderers see. A vast, endless labyrinth resembling the back rooms of office buildings or retail stores. The entire level is characterized by oppressive monotony.

### Visual Appearance
- **Wallpaper:** Sickly yellow, old-fashioned pattern (like grandparent's house)
- **Carpet:** Moist, soggy brown/beige synthetic material saturated with various liquids
- **Lighting:** Irregularly placed fluorescent lights buzzing constantly overhead
- **Color Palette:** Yellow-beige monotony—after a while "it all just blends together into one big yellow blur"
- **Overall Feel:** Utterly desolate, psychologically oppressive

### Environmental Hazards & Sub-Areas

1. **Archways** - Pale walls with archway openings, typically found at dead ends or transition zones. Unlike most of Level 0, these sections stay put — they don't shift behind travelers, making them one of the few reliable navigation reference points in the level.
2. **Pillar Rooms** - Massive chambers with pillars arranged in a grid, stretching for miles. Carpet here is shallower/drier, making these zones actually suitable for resting — but the paths between pillars shift asymmetrically behind you the moment you look away.
3. **Pit Clusters** - Grid-arranged holes descending into pitch darkness. No wanderer has ever reported surviving a fall. Extremely dangerous to anyone already fatigued or dehydrated, since exhaustion makes the grid pattern harder to track underfoot.
4. **Blackout Zones** - Completely unlit sections, rough-textured walls, ankle-deep fluid pooling on the floor. Layout shifts constantly here too. Survival advice from wanderers: sprint toward any visible light, or follow the sound of buzzing (it means a working fluorescent panel, hence a "normal" section, is close).
5. **Red Rooms** - See dedicated section below. Do not enter.
6. **The Manila Room** - See dedicated section below. The one safe harbor in the whole level.
7. **The Carpet Fluid** - Continuous Berber-style synthetic carpet, saturated with an unnerving mixture of saltwater, brackish water, almond water, "liquid pain," formalin, and DNA-containing slurries.
   - WARNING: Do not attempt to consume floor chunks, even in desperation.

### Red Rooms (Critical Danger Zone)

Red Rooms are disconnected pockets within Level 0 that form **inescapable closed loops**. They are the one hazard on Level 0 more dangerous than the level itself.

**Warning signs before you're fully inside:**
- Wallpaper color shifting from yellow toward red
- Peeling wallpaper revealing crimson underneath
- Carpet texture changing from slick/moist to **coarse and sticky**
- Lighting shifting to a dim, reddish, darkroom-like quality
- Colonies of mushrooms and black mold appearing in corners — a sign you're already too close

**Once inside:**
- The entrance you used **disappears** the moment you're through
- There is no known exit apart from death
- Emotional effects hit immediately and escalate: extreme paranoia, anxiety, claustrophobia, disgust, despair — wanderers have compared the emotional progression to the five stages of grief
- The sudden violent break from Level 0's droning monotony makes the psychological impact worse, not better

**Design takeaway:** Red Rooms should never be survivable once truly entered — they exist as a one-way trap, a place the player learns to recognize and flee from by its visual tells alone, not something to fight or wait out.

### The Manila Room (Safe Point Exception)

A small, rare room that looks distinctly different from the rest of Level 0 (manila-colored, not yellow) and is **immune to the Isolation Effect**.

- Always appears in the **same relative location** for every wanderer who finds it — one of the only fixed points in an otherwise ever-shifting level
- Acts as a meeting point: wanderers who separately reach it can actually see and interact with each other, unlike anywhere else on the level
- Contains **M.E.G. (Major Explorer Group) documentation** — survival guides left behind by past explorers
- Roughly 36% of wanderers who found the Manila Room's documentation reported it was essential to eventually escaping Level 0
- Because the rest of Level 0 keeps reshuffling around it, actually stumbling onto the Manila Room a second time is exceedingly rare — it's a landmark you may only ever see once

### Atmospheric & Psychological Effects
- **Fluorescent Hum:** The constant grating buzz induces migraines and psychological deterioration. Volume fluctuates randomly and can be loud enough to cause hearing damage.
- **Isolation Effect:** Wanderers — even those who entered together — cannot find each other. Shouting, leaving notes, damaging walls to mark a trail: none of it works. The Manila Room is the sole exception.
- **Peripheral Shift:** Layout warps, stretches, or rearranges whenever it's not being directly observed. Days of already-traveled corridor can vanish or reroute in an instant. Wanderers report catching movement at the edge of vision that isn't there when they turn to look.
- **Unexplained Sounds:** Beyond the hum, wanderers report indistinct whispering, voices that sound like people they know calling out, and scratching from inside the walls. Source unconfirmed.
- **Psychological Breakdown:** Paranoia, claustrophobia, delirium from endless sameness — compounded further and faster near Red Rooms.

### Items Found on Level 0

Level 0 is not entirely barren — supplies exist, they're just extremely sparse relative to how much ground you have to cover.

- **Object 1 — Almond Water:** The most common survival item found across the Backrooms, and the primary water source on Level 0. Found in plastic bottles, metal cans, glass wine bottles, cartons, water fountains, or occasionally pooled/concentrated in small bodies of liquid. Sourced from cabinets, drawers, bags, "infinite fridges," or simply lying on the floor. Sweet almond/vanilla taste with a hint of rose water. Effects: rehydrates, cures minor ailments, provides a small well-being/focus boost, and can neutralize certain hostile-entity effects. Valuable enough that Backrooms communities use it as informal currency.
- **Object 2 — Level Keys:** Extremely rare. Each numbered/anomalous level has a corresponding key, physically unique to that level, marked with a runic symbol matching the door it opens. Not something you'd realistically find loose on Level 0, but part of the wider mythology of how deliberate travel between levels works (as opposed to accidental noclipping).
- **Object 3 — Smiler Repellent:** An extremely rare liquid that repels Smilers specifically. Origin and composition unknown even in-universe — treated as a precious, fought-over resource specifically because it counters one of the most dangerous entities in the Backrooms.

### Survival Mechanics
- **Duration:** Most wanderers die from dehydration or starvation, not entities — Level 0 is nicknamed "safe" precisely because the level itself, not its inhabitants, is the main killer.
- **Time Distortion:** Difficult to track time; confusion about how long you've been wandering.
- **Scarce Resources:** The level is not literally foodless/waterless (see Items above), but supplies are so sparse relative to its infinite scale that most wanderers never find enough.
- **Difficulty Rating:** Safe (1/10 threat level) — the irony being that a "safe" rating still has an extremely high fatality rate, just from attrition rather than violence.

### Sub-Levels of Level 0

Level 0 has several documented sub-levels branching off it, reached under specific unusual conditions rather than the standard flickering-wall exit:

- **Level 0.1** ("Danger Zone" / "Deep Emptiness") - Interpretations vary by wiki source: one version is an extremely long hallway of doorways (most leading to empty rooms, presumed length in the hundreds of thousands of miles); another depicts it as visually identical to Level 0 but bathed entirely in red light.
- **Level 0.2** ("Room Maintenance") - Resembles Level 0 but noticeably less maze-like; some accounts describe a red-carpet aesthetic that appears to be deliberately constructed and spread by M.E.G., rather than a natural Backrooms formation.
- **Level 0.3** ("The Icy Rooms") - A colder variant; details vary across wiki sources.
- **Level 0.7** ("The Reminiscence District") - A nostalgia-themed variant.

*(Sub-level lore is the least consistent part of the mythology across wikis — treat these as flavor/inspiration rather than fixed canon.)*

### Escape Route
- Look for **flickering walls** - these lead to Level 1
- Must navigate through the endless maze to find one
- No other confirmed exits exist from Level 0 proper (sub-levels are reached through separate, rarer anomalies)

## Levels 1 Through 5: Full Documentation

These are the levels most wanderers pass through directly after Level 0. Difficulty generally trends upward, but not evenly — Level 4 is actually safer than Level 1, and Level 3 is a spike in both danger and lore density.

### Level 1: "The Habitable Zone"

**Appearance:** An endless chimera between an underground parking lot and an abandoned warehouse — bland discolored concrete, occasional massive pillars. Split into two geometries: **Halls** (open, non-Euclidean — distances lie to you) and **Corridors** (tight, labyrinthine, but spatially normal). Six distinct sectors exist: Aquila (grey parking-lot, main M.E.G. presence), Gild (colorful, crate-rich), Gothic (arches, circular pillars), Ouroboros (perpetually under construction by mysterious workers), Garden (overgrown vegetation that slowly transforms humans into plants), and Fabled (historic wood/vermilion architecture retrofitted with modern tech).

**Signature hazard — Flickering:** Random total blackout events. Creatures emerge specifically during these windows. Survival protocol: feel along the walls toward green exit signs, avoid making light or noise.

**Crates:** Wooden boxes scattered throughout containing food, tools, clothing, medical supplies, and Backrooms-specific items (entity-detecting jades, Squirt Guns, Warpberries). They disappear when not being observed — you have to be thorough the moment you find one.

**Entities:** Skin-Stealers (wear victim skin as disguise), Dullers (no-clip predators), Smilers, Clumps (fused limb clusters, fast), Hounds, Wranglers (hypnotic snake-things), Crawlers (mind-infecting fungus in unsealed liquids), Nguithr'xurh (ceiling-dwelling insectoids), Arms (emerge from vents during blackouts), Growlers, Wretches.

**Groups:** M.E.G. Base Alpha (largest settlement, Aquila Sector), Trader's Keep (B.N.T.G. barter hub, Gild Sector), Hippocrates-1 (free medical care, Gothic Sector), Tom's Diner (social hub/restaurant), Camp Amber (nomadic educators), Cornucopia (Justice Seeker enforcement HQ).

**Difficulty:** 1/10 — "Safe and Secure." Entry from Level 0's flickering walls; exits mostly toward Level 2.

### Level 2: "Abandoned Utility Halls"

**Appearance:** Infinite but strictly Euclidean maintenance tunnels — corridors bend only at 45° angles, lengths measure in multiples of ~5 meters. Weathered concrete/brick walls, chalky texture that stains on contact. Non-functional industrial debris everywhere (pipes, machinery, oil drums) suggesting the level "regenerated" after some past event. Lights run in **series circuits**, not parallel — damage one panel and a whole 3x3–6x6 grid around it goes dark.

**Behind the (mostly locked) doors:** warehouse spaces, looping office-like hallways that return to their own entrance, single empty rooms, or — rarely — **infinite void rooms** with no light or sound beyond the doorway.

**Signature event — The Blackout Crisis:** A B.N.T.G. battery failure once shorted the entire level for two years, killing all machinery and lighting, rupturing pipes, and awakening long-locked void rooms. Level 2 never fully recovered economically; most groups abandoned it afterward for better-off levels.

**Major historical residents — The Macchina:** an ancient cybernetic entity race (~1000 BC) who industrialized the level, then suffered catastrophic population collapse around 1850. Their leftover stripped machinery explains the level's current derelict-industrial look.

**Groups (mostly historical/reduced):** Aiding Stragglers (humanitarian group, overrun by Hounds in 2013, later reformed as "The Survivors"), Followers of Jerry (maintain a door-based shortcut to their Level 274 base; exploited the Blackout to kidnap wanderers), a brief M.E.G. presence before relocating to Level 3, and B.N.T.G.'s Office Space EL3A — their main hub, defended and still active, complete with an abandoned narrow-gauge tramway.

**Entities:** Smilers (surged during the Blackout), Deathmoths, Clumps (among the deadliest — ambush at bends), Hounds, Facelings (predate the Macchina), Wranglers (burrow and snatch), Nguithr'xurh, Death Rats (harmless scavengers), Cannibal Cuisines, Growlers, Jerry (Entity 7, passive but dangerous if provoked), Photoshop (Entity 104, appears only near PCs).

**Difficulty:** 3/10.

---

### Level 3: "Electrical Station"

**Appearance:** An old electrical facility — inconsistent brick coloring, dusty concrete, dim fluorescents. Level 0's hum persists here, subtler. Corridor sizes swing wildly, from crawlspaces (5ft tall, 14in wide) to areas the size of a mall. Ambient temperature 66–93°F, often stuffy.

**Signature hazard — Indestructible Bars:** metal barriers blocking huge sections of the level. Nothing has ever breached them — not chainsaws, explosives, acid, specialized weapons, nor reality-bending devices. A handful have lockpickable gates; most don't.

**Black Sludge:** thick neurotoxin in the level's pipes. Scalding (2nd-degree burns on contact), and its fumes cause severe brain damage if inhaled. Its molecular structure doesn't match anything terrestrial.

**Purpification:** an extremely rare phenomenon (17 documented cases) where a dead entity partially no-clips into a wall and turns entirely purple, radiating gamma energy. No human has been affected — yet. A cryptic inscription found near one reads: *"My beautiful creation, seeing you in such a perverted state deeply hurts me."*

**Sanctums:** cathedral-like rooms, Greco-Roman architecture with gothic elements, each containing statues of an unidentified angelic deity. Every entity on the level is inexplicably terrified of these rooms and won't follow wanderers inside — the closest thing to a guaranteed safe room in the Backrooms.

**Room types:** Generator Rooms (Almond Water and Firesalt caches), Assembly Lines (endless conveyor belts, the level's main resource source — 70% of all M.E.G. supplies trace back here), Boiler Rooms (source of the black sludge), Sanctums.

**Entities (notably hyper-intelligent here, unlike elsewhere):** Hounds (coordinate hunts via barking, deliberately maim rather than kill to prolong suffering), Facelings (all hostile here, weapon-trained, door-opening), Deathmoths (both sexes aggressive, immune to repellent, three empresses reportedly cooperate and may speak English), Skin-Stealers (fluent English, disguises last days instead of hours), Smilers (evolved eyelids/lips to hide their glow), Clumps (up to 8ft, highly intelligent, wield weapons), Death Rats (Capybara-sized, set traps mimicking M.E.G. gear, literate).

**Base Gamma (M.E.G.):** the largest human-built structure in the Backrooms — a fortified mall-and-residential complex with public, residential, and administrative sectors, full biometric security, and a documented history of four major breaches (2014–2019) involving Clumps, an assassin Skin-Stealer, an unexplained Purpified entity, and a Deathmoth swarm. Also present: Storage Unit (B.N.T.G. warehouse/pawn shop), Blue Salvation (a small, unsettling Followers-of-Jerry outpost — avoid), Scylla Colony (a U.E.C. group running inhumane entity experiments — strongly avoid).

**Difficulty:** unrated numerically in the source, but treated as high-stakes due to Base Gamma's defensive posture and the hyper-intelligent entities.

### Level 4: "Abandoned Office"

**Appearance:** An empty office building, nearly devoid of furniture. Windows that are fully blacked out are normal; **windows that aren't blacked out are traps** — avoid them. Water coolers, vending machines, and fountains are scattered around.

**Entities:** sparse — just Hounds and Dullers confirmed, with a single unverified Smiler report. This low entity density is exactly why wanderers like to stop here.

**Resources:** the best place on the early levels to restock Almond Water (coolers, machines, fountains) before pushing on.

**Groups:** M.E.G. Base Omega (second major M.E.G. base, near the Level 5/6 entrances, heavily guarded), Amor Incrementum (small hostile agricultural cult growing mold species, trades only for religious items), T.B.D. (tiny group hoarding oddities, trades only for strange objects).

**Entry:** noclip from The Hub, elevator from Level 3, unlocked door from Level 2, or (rarely) falling through the pit in Level 283.
**Exit:** office stairways/elevators to Levels 5 and 6, occasional elevator back to Level 3, rare basement stairs to Level 71. Exits can vanish if you lose line of sight on them.

**Difficulty:** 1/10 — one of the safest levels in the entire Backrooms.

### Level 5: "Terror Hotel"

**Appearance:** An infinite hotel styled after the 1930s, furnished like the 1920s, and — unusually for the Backrooms — kept pristine and clean. Smooth jazz plays constantly from vintage turntables of unknown origin. Mahogany-red and gold wallpaper, with some reports of faces hidden in the pattern.

**Unsettling atmosphere despite the tidy look:** distant party chatter with no visible party, incoherent whispers from behind you, phantom shoulder-taps, and the sense of being watched by the painted figures on the walls.

**Three (or four) main areas:**
- **The Main Hall** — the bulk of the level: antique furnishings, mixed wood/marble/carpet flooring, non-sequential room numbers on gold placards, elevators serving floors 1–382 (floors 1–12 loop back into Level 5; floor 13+ means death or disappearance).
- **The Beverly Room ("The Eternal Ballroom")** — a central hub with doors on every surface (walls, floor, ceiling) in seemingly random counts, one table with drinks and an unfinished Mahjong game that wanderers feel an inexplicable compulsion not to disturb.
- **The Boiler Room** — vintage industrial machinery, pipes full of leaking Almond Water, oppressive heat, and whispers reportedly coming from inside the furnaces.
- **The Lobby Room (unconfirmed)** — disputed to even exist. Anyone who's found it only ever says "You're not supposed to know," and loses all memory of it within weeks. M.E.G. investigation ongoing; theory that many wanderers have already passed through without remembering.

**Entities:** Deathmoths (most abundant, nest throughout), Hounds, Skin-Stealers, Watchers, Woodlins, Nguithr'xurh, Growlers, Death Rats, "Ants" (unconfirmed), a Wrangler reported in the Boiler Room, and **Entity 26 "Samantha"** — occasionally wanders the Main Hall; approach only with high-quality meat as an offering.

**Groups:** M.E.G. Outpost "Housekeeping," Homely Hotel (a genuine hotel-service operation offering room/food service to wanderers), The Originals (31 members who entered the Backrooms between 1300–1940, including historical figures like Amelia Earhart and Captain Edward Smith of the Titanic — only accepts members from that era), The Lost Hall Society (an archive-keeper group of unknown origin holding maps and historical records in Level 5.3).

**Difficulty:** 2/5 — relatively safe, but the psychological unease is the real cost of staying here.

## Major Entities & Threats

### Entity 3: "Smilers"

**Appearance:**
- Pure darkness with a crescent of glowing teeth
- Reflective eyes gleaming in the black
- Amorphous body that can shift and move
- Most commonly found in dark/unlit zones

**Behavior:**
- Remain still most of the time
- Sudden noises or motions trigger aggression
- Attracted to light initially but retreat if illuminated
- **Critical weakness:** Keep a flashlight on them and they will retreat
- **Critical danger:** If your light dies, they strike instantly

**Biology:**
- Two documented organs: 
  - Nigrum ignem (unidentified spectral pitch-black substance)
  - Ardenti risu (luminescent substance forming eyes and teeth)

**Threat Level:** Extreme in darkness; manageable if you have reliable light

---

### Wretches

**Origins:**
- Once human wanderers
- Mutated by prolonged exposure to fluorescent hum and Level 0 itself
- Have forgotten their previous identities—only hunger remains

**Appearance:**
- Reddish-brown skin, dry and flaky
- Covered in holes and pustules
- Skeletal appearance with surprising strength
- Common mutations:
  - Misplaced eyes
  - Disjointed or excess limbs
  - Fleshy, skeletal "wings"

**Behavior:**
- Drawn to sound (walking, talking, equipment noise)
- Aggressive hunters
- Will pursue anything that makes noise

**Threat Level:** High; requires stealth to avoid

## Backrooms Mechanics & Rules

### Noclipping
- The method of entry into the Backrooms
- Occurs by exiting reality in certain architectural spaces
- Usually accidental, sometimes purposeful
- Very difficult to reverse

### Levels & Navigation
- Levels are self-contained spaces connected by hidden exits
- Some exits are obvious (flickering walls, doors), others hidden
- Each level has distinct rules and physics
- Getting "deeper" generally means more danger

### Time Distortion
- Time behaves strangely in the Backrooms
- Hours may feel like minutes or vice versa
- Clocks and watches often malfunction

### The Hum
- A constant low-frequency vibration present in many levels
- Most pronounced in Level 0
- Can cause physical and psychological damage over time

### Resources
- Some levels contain water and food
- Level 0 has sparse but real supplies (see Almond Water above) — scarcity, not total absence, is the danger
- Certain levels are more "habitable" than others

---

## Design Philosophy for Game Implementation

### What Makes the Backrooms Compelling?

1. **Liminal Space Uncanny Valley** - Familiar spaces that feel wrong
2. **Isolation** - Alone, lost, no clear way out
3. **Infinite Scale** - Endless corridors, impossible to map
4. **Sensory Overload** - Buzzing lights, wet carpets, strange smells
5. **Slow Psychological Deterioration** - Time spent in the Backrooms breaks the mind
6. **Resource Scarcity** - Hunger, thirst, exhaustion
7. **Unknown Threats** - Not just monsters, but the space itself is hostile

### Audio Design Importance
- Fluorescent hum is a core mechanical element
- Silence is terrifying (absence of familiar hum)
- Creatures use sound-based detection
- Player must manage noise level

### Visual Design Importance
- Color monotony is a feature, not a bug
- Repetition and sameness are psychological tools
- Contrast (sudden changes) creates dread
- Darkness without light is deadly (Smilers)
